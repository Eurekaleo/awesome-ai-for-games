AI and Foundation Models for Game Design

How generative and multimodal models help designers explore levels, worlds, assets, narratives, rules, and mechanics.

Direct answer

AI and foundation models for game design help people propose, explore, compare, and revise game concepts and content. They can generate levels, maps, quests, stories, dialogue structures, visual or 3D assets, rules, mechanics, and complete design alternatives from text, sketches, examples, or partial specifications. Foundation models broaden these workflows by bringing pretrained knowledge and flexible language or multimodal interfaces to procedural content generation and mixed-initiative design tools. The central challenge is not producing an attractive artifact; it is producing something that functions inside a game and remains controllable by a designer. A visual asset needs geometry, topology, animation, collision, or placement information. A level must be playable and satisfy structural constraints. A proposed rule must interact coherently with the rest of the system. Useful tools should expose alternatives, preserve intent across revisions, and support editing rather than forcing designers to accept a single opaque output. Evaluation therefore considers validity, diversity, constraint satisfaction, novelty, controllability, editability, and effects on real design work and player experience. Design claims should also remain distinct from implementation claims: describing a mechanic is not the same as building and testing it in an engine.

This topic is surveyed in AI for Games in the Foundation Model Era.

Evidence

What should evaluation establish?

Evaluation should test whether outputs satisfy game and designer constraints, remain meaningfully different, and support revision. User studies are strongest when they follow authentic design work and measure correction effort, retained alternatives, and downstream player experience.

  • Validity and constraint satisfaction
  • Diversity without trivial variation
  • Designer control and editability
  • Effect on workflow and player experience
